# Build record: decisions, checks and open items

Dates: 2026-11-14 to 2026-11-15. The work was built on the branch `apartment-finder` (head `9ffde6e`) and merged into `main` on 2026-11-15. Nothing is published.

This is the hand-over record for three plans built by subagents under one coordinator (Claude): the apartment finder, the building spotlight, and the complaint outcomes. While the plans ran, the coordinator decided every conflict and ambiguity instead of stopping to ask. Each decision was logged as a ruling with what it costs if it was wrong. All 120 are listed at the end. The ones most worth Gabe's eye come first.

## Decisions most worth a second look

### Names

1. **When OpenStreetMap and the city's registration disagree about a building's name, no name is shown.** Nine buildings, including 1601 Royal Crest Dr, the largest record on the site. A web check found the city's name current at five of the eight first found, OpenStreetMap's at one, neither at one. Cost if wrong: those buildings lose a title, and a search for either name finds nothing.
2. **A city registration name is a title only when it is on an approved list** (`app/pipeline/snapshots/title-review.json`). Eleven are approved, each confirmed against a current listing or the property's own site. Fourteen more wait for review and show their address; the build prints them. The web check found 13 of 25 city names current and 11 out of date.
3. **About 808 OpenStreetMap names are unverified beyond a random sample of 12,** in which 10 matched, 1 was a former name and 1 could not be found at its address. Shipping them, checking them all or holding them back is not decided. See `docs/2026-11-14-names-check.md`.
4. **OpenStreetMap's Open Database License** applies to those names. The spotlight design asks Gabe to confirm he is comfortable with it before names ship. Not confirmed.

### Phones

5. **While a record is open on a phone, the title block, search and filter bar are hidden.** They return on close. Cost if wrong: search and filters cannot be reached until the record is closed.
6. **On a phone the Repeat Offender chip on a card is only its coral dot.** The words stay for screen readers and on hover. The chip was spilling onto the next card.
7. **Phone landscape has no layout of its own.** At 812 by 375 the desktop layout applies and the model is out of view. Not fixed.

### Cards and words

8. **Cards grew 18 px** to fit an outcome line, and that line uses short forms: "119 confirmed, 78 none found". The full labels are in the hover text and the panel.
9. **Filter buttons read Building, Grounds, Zoning, Permits,** each with its full name and meaning on hover. The full names would not fit on 1280 and 1366 px screens.
10. **"In the city's records, look for Structure Condition"** replaces the approved sketch's "The city calls this Structure Condition". The city's actual text is "Structure Condition Violation(s)", so "calls this" was not exactly true, and printing the city's full text would put the word violation beside a complaint type.
11. **Before its photo arrives, the panel header is steel to slate,** not the mock's pale fog, and the title has a soft shadow. A two-line title was unreadable on the pale ground.
12. **Card titles keep to one line.** Two lines overflow the fixed card.

### Counting

13. **A complaint with a violation case number is Violation confirmed even when the city's last recorded outcome says no violation was found.** One complaint in the data does this (9024 Northgate Blvd, opened 2026-03-02). The city's phrase is shown under the label, so the contradiction is visible. Counting it as "No violation found" instead is a one-rule change.
14. **The record panel is the building's full record whatever type filter is on.** The matching category opens by itself, so the card's number is the first thing seen.
15. **A building's own page shows the 3-year record,** with no period control.
16. **Recorded problems keep the city's section reference,** as in "General (§504.1)". The city's dataset has no plainer name for the part of the code, and without the reference "General" hid seven different sections. A section number means little to a renter.
17. **An outcome phrase the site does not recognize is shown as nothing,** not as "Other update" as the design said. There are none today, and the build lists any that appear.

### Behavior

18. **The turn around a picked building stops on a key press and after one full revolution,** as well as on a touch. The design said only "until touched". A keyboard user could not stop it, and a tab left open turned forever.
19. **The nightly build refuses to publish** when more than 3% of complaints have a blank outcome, fewer than 85% are closed, fewer than 1,000 recorded problems arrive or more than 5% of them are skipped. A refused build keeps yesterday's site. The latest readings: 0.2%, 96.7%, 5,373 and 0%.
